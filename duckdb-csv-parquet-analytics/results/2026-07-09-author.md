# Author's run — 2026-07-09

**Machine:** AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (12 cores / 24 threads), 62 GB RAM, NVMe SSD, Ubuntu (Linux 6.17, x86_64).
**Versions:** DuckDB CLI 1.5.4, python duckdb 1.5.4, pandas 2.2.3, polars 1.17.1, pyarrow 18.1.0.
**Data:** NYC TLC yellow taxi, all 12 months of 2024 (`--full`), 41,169,720 rows, 661 MB on disk.

## Stage 1 — direct query, no import
`SELECT count(*), avg(fare_amount), avg(trip_distance)` straight off the Parquet glob: 41,169,720 trips, avg fare $19.27, avg 4.98 miles. Wall time **0.06s**, zero `CREATE TABLE`.

## Stage 2 — DuckDB vs Pandas vs Polars (41.2M rows, median of 5)

| operation | pandas | duckdb | polars |
|-----------|-------:|-------:|-------:|
| filter    | 127 ms |  23 ms | 157 ms |
| group-by  | 893 ms |  20 ms | 272 ms |
| join      | 6795 ms | 20 ms | 1316 ms |

DuckDB is fastest on all three on this 24-thread box: ~45x faster than pandas on group-by and ~340x on the join. Polars trails DuckDB here (single query, cold thread pool at this scale) but stays well ahead of pandas on the join.

## Stage 3 — CSV vs Parquet scan (identical data, one month)
Same selective `WHERE fare_amount > 20 GROUP BY PULocationID` query:
- Parquet (48 MB): **10.7 ms**
- CSV (286 MB): **149.5 ms**
- Parquet **14x faster** via projection + predicate pushdown.

## Stage 4 — out-of-core sort under a 2GB memory_limit
Full sort of all 41.2M rows on two columns, materialized, with `memory_limit='2GB'`:
- Completed in **6.04s**
- **Peak spill to disk: 8.8 GB** in the temp directory.

The sort's working set is far larger than the 2GB budget, so DuckDB spilled to disk and finished anyway. This is the out-of-core behavior the post describes.
